Kaleidoscope Digital

A mockup of the Bip 6 canvas: the watch side only uses what the watch canvas has (drawRect, drawCircle, drawLine, drawImage x / y / w / h; no clip, rotate or flip), at the speed estimated from measurements. Shapes wake: they fly in while the kaleidoscope turns (3 s) and drift to rest (2 s). Picture-widget wake: zooms in (stored frames) while turning in, then drifts to rest. Canvas-picture wake: the slices zoom from 2× to 1× (5 s, slowing at the end), then hold still.

Your seed picture, tiled 3 × 3 with mirrored copies (more variety, no seams). The red slice is the window cut out for this wake; every wake picks another window.

Red lines = the mirrors (arms = mirror lines; slices = 2 × arms). The shapes only exist in the shaded slice. The watch draws that slice in every slice around the centre: every second copy is flipped, so a shape touching a red line meets its own reflection. That's the kaleidoscope. In Shapes mode the red lines turn with it (the slow rotation); in picture mode nothing turns. The timeline bar shows where you are.

Computer (before install): cuts one window from your picture into a slice picture plus its flipped copy, once per zoom frame (2 pictures per frame). Watch: one native picture widget per slice; every second one shows the flipped copy; each is turned to its slice by the widget's own angle around the screen centre, so turning the whole kaleidoscope costs nothing. Wake: the picture zooms in (set below) while the kaleidoscope turns in from 90°, then both drift to rest (5 s). The zoom is stored frames (a picture widget can't resize), so it moves in steps: a smaller zoom makes each step smaller. The turn is free and smooth. Speed: 8 turned picture widgets measured 24 fps; for 12 the number is an estimate.

Computer (before install): cuts one window from your picture into one set of triangle pictures (one per slice, already turned and flipped, since the watch can't). Watch: pastes that same set every frame with drawImage, changing only x, y, w, h: all slices start at 2× around the centre and shrink to 1×, so the picture pours in from the edge and the slices always touch. Nothing turns (the canvas can't). Speed estimate 17 ms + 3 ms per paste; pasting resized pictures every frame is not measured yet.

Estimate from the real watch: 17 ms per frame for the background + about 1.1 ms per shape drawn (41 draws measured 16–17 fps on a Bip 6). Size doesn't change the speed; the number of shapes does. Each shape is drawn once per slice, so more arms cost more: 6 arms × 4 shapes = 49 draws, the same as 4 arms × 6 shapes.

From the Lospec palette list (via the elm-lospec data) and neon sets from Filmora, Media.io, HubSpot and Color Meanings, filtered for saturation and contrast on black, without magenta, chroma-key green and raw neon. Background: black; a lighter grey only when a pattern's colours would be too dark to see. Rows: 100%, 60%, 20% opacity (pre-mixed, the watch canvas has no transparency). Click one to use it.
